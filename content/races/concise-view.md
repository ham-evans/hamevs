*David Evans, aka Don Evans*
*March 2, 2011*

My full name is David Donald Evans.

I was born in Clovis, New Mexico, on December 28, 1930. My father was Charles Hamilton Evans and my Mother was Grace Anna Myers. We lived in a modest home at 1024 W. Main Street in a newer part of Clovis, and I was born in the living room with a local doctor in attendance. Home deliveries were commonly done then.

At that time I had two older sisters, De Aun and Lenora, and an older brother, Carroll, as well as an older Step-Brother and Step-Sister, Charles Jr, and LeElla.

I am unsure of the immigration history of my parents or grandparents. We do know that Evans is a Welsh name and that Welsh miners were brought over to work in the coal mines of Pennsylvania during the early times of the colonies. My Father, Charles, was originally from Georgia, but had moved to Texas where he met my Mother. They lived in Cookville, Texas for several years before moving to Clovis in 1928.

Charles was elected County Treasurer of Curry County and served in that capacity for a few years. In 1935 the family moved to Farmington, New Mexico, where I spent most of my childhood. We lived in a very modest home not far from the downtown section initially, but then moved out to a farm on East Main Street, about 2 miles from the downtown section. It was referred to as the Calloway place since the owner, Mr Calloway, had lived there and continued to live in a small home adjacent to the larger farm house in which we lived.

## Childhood

As were many families during this period we were basically poor, although as a child I really didn't notice it since we always had food and clothing (much of it hand-me-downs, which was the norm in larger families). I had a fun childhood building toy roads outside and playing cars, playing games outside (like hide-and-seek), and generally playing with friends from the neighborhood, sometimes until it was too dark to play outside. Of course we did not have access to television nor videogames, so we spent more time outside. We would also play board games and such inside during bad weather. During the evenings we would often listen to our favorite programs, such as "Fibber McGee and Molly" (funny) or "The Shadow" (scary).

We lived at two different homes in Farmington, both with enough land for a fairly large garden, which supplied ample fruits and vegetables for our use. We were expected to do chores and our share of work feeding animals, pulling weeds or whatever else needed to be done, including my most-hated task, cleaning out the chicken coop! We always had a cow and chickens, and therefore had a supply of fresh eggs and whole milk as well as a fried chicken dinner (meaning the mid-day meal), typically on Sunday after Church.

Originally, when we lived at the Calloway place, we walked the two miles daily to the nearest school, including those days with snow on the ground. One had to live further away for bus service at that time. Later we moved closer to town at 620 East Main Street, which was a short walk to school and also I could ride my bicycle to the Saturday movie and to the store for food, etc.

Without computers and cell phones our social circles were just friends from school or the neighborhoods, which we would meet with at our home or theirs to play, and later to go to movies and school events together. When we were old enough to drive cars we would often cruise Main Street after a movie or something or go to a drive-in for a snack.

## Youth

I was a good student in high school and found that I really liked mathematics and science. I was also active in student government and was voted to be student body president during my senior year. I played in a band in high school as well, and chose the drums since we couldn't afford a regular instrument. While in high school I took a job at the Farmington Drug store. I started out as a soda jerk but later became basically a pharmacist assistant, filling prescriptions which of course the pharmacist reviewed before releasing them. As a result of this experience I enrolled in the pharmacy program at the University of New Mexico.

## Diseases

As a younger child I had both measles and mumps. While visiting a cousin in Albuquerque, New Mexico I was diagnosed with a case of scarlet fever when I was about 11 years old. It left me with a "heart murmur," a condition that prevented me from playing active sports in junior high or high school, so I became a referee and later team manager for our sports teams.

## World War II

During World War II (I was 11 years old when it started.) I became fascinated with the progress of the war and avidly read the newspapers and listened to the radio for reports. I was especially fascinated by military aviation, reading books and that sort of thing. As a result of this interest I entered pilot training during the Korean War era, and became a jet fighter pilot just before an armistice was signed with the North Koreans. I never served overseas or in combat.

## Mt St Helens

I don't recall where I was when Mount St. Helens erupted and it had no real effect on my life.

## Stories

One of my favorite stories is from an early experience at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory where I was employed after graduation from the University of Colorado. I was an entry engineer assigned to the propulsion research section. I was observing a test of an experimental device that was known to malfunction on occasion. We observed a test behind very thick concrete walls and "bulletproof" glass. I was allowed to observe from a window as the countdown proceeded down to zero. At that time the device exploded, scattering parts in all directions. It was fascinating to watch, however when I turned around to check on my coworkers I found that they were all on the floor! As a new engineer I didn't realize that this was the best thing to do and was obviously embarrassed, but it makes a great story.

## Generations

I am sure there are loftier things that the new generation should be aware of, but from my personal standpoint I think developing an appreciation for the earlier generations and passing this on to future generations would be a very good thing to do.
