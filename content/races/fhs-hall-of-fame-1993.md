## Hall of Fame

Gaining momentum from the success of our Athletic and Activity Hall of Fame program, Farmington High School organized a Hall of Fame committee to honor graduates of FHS who have significantly distinguished themselves in their chosen fields of endeavor. The committee was charged with the responsibility of determining induction criteria, receiving nominations from the public, selecting this year's inductees, and planning and financing the induction.

This, our inaugural class, includes three teachers, one of whom is being inducted posthumously, and five graduates who have impacted society in a variety of ways. Our plan is to continue to induct worthy individuals on years alternating with the Athletic and Activity Hall. Nominations for future inductees may be submitted throughout the year by mailing them to the FHS principal.

May fourth was selected as the date for our first induction to celebrate National Teacher's Day in honor of those whose work so often goes unnoticed.

## Farmington High: A Special Force

*By Lindley J. Stiles, Class of 1931*

Where three Rivers flow together,
Cultures merge in Farmington High,
Rain or shine, in any weather,
Currents compete as they pass by.

As tiny raindrops, children grow,
Nourished by nature and human kind,
Blending all cultures they come to know,
Gaining in body, soul and mind.

The course of a mighty river
Is shaped by all that makes it flow,
So with people, each a giver,
Direction comes from all they know.

But rivers choose to run downhill,
While humans strive for higher ground;
Progress requires collective will,
Common commitments must abound.

Shaping growth in human currents,
Is the Force of Farmington High,
Making pathways and detours,
Pushing all to reach for the sky.

## Newspaper Clipping

*"FHS announces quintet as hall of fame inductees" — Jeff Graves, Staff Writer*

"This is the first return to Mars in 17 years. I'm proud to have managed it." — David Evans

You don't have to be a rocket scientist to make the FHS Distinguished Achievement Hall of Fame as an alumni. But it doesn't hurt, as one 1949 graduate found out.

David "Don" Evans, a jet propulsion engineer for more than 25 years, is among five Farmington High School graduates who will be inducted into the new hall of fame on May 4.

An Idaho state attorney general, a U.S. Navy vice admiral, an oil executive and an author also made the grade for the hall, which was created as an alternate to the Athletics and Activity Hall of Fame.

Class president at FHS and an honor graduate at the University of New Mexico, Evans took flight at a young age and never looked back.

Evans flew jet fighters in the U.S. Air Force before graduating as the outstanding aeronautical engineering student from the University of Colorado in 1958.

He went on to garner praise and the outstanding leadership medal at NASA. He managed the Mars Observer Project, a massive NASA undertaking involving the multiyear orbit.

"This is the first return to Mars in 17 years," Evans said of the $1 billion project from his Pasadena, Calif., home. "I'm proud to have managed it."

The unmanned craft is on its way to Mars and should arrive there in August, where it will begin five years of mapping the surface and producing high-resolution photographs.

Larry EchoHawk, Class of 1966, became the first Native American elected to a state constitutional office when he was elected in Idaho in 1991. Newsweek magazine called him a promising Western and national leader. The grandson of a Pawnee scout, EchoHawk has taught law at several colleges and was awarded the Martin Luther King Medal from George Washington University.

High rank and a Distinguished Service Medal with a gold star in the U.S. Navy was in the future when Kleber Masterson graduated in 1925. He played on the first FHS football team before graduating and shipping out to the U.S. Naval Academy. During his 39-year Navy career, Masterson survived the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor while serving aboard the U.S.S. Arizona and received promotion to vice admiral in 1964.

A 1929 graduate, Hal Nabors worked his way up the corporate ladder from his early days in the oil business as a pick-and-shovel man to the prime of his 40-year career when he served as president of Dubai Petroleum Company. Listed in 1966 among "Who's Who in America," Nabors had 44 residences in 11 states and two countries. After retiring, the energetic Nabors organized the Arizona Petroleum Club and Conoco Club of Arizona.

Lindley Stiles graduated in 1931. He went on to a 44-year career in education, from 1935 to 1979. Stiles began as a teacher in Colorado and advanced to dean of the School of Education at the University of Wisconsin, where he served from 1955 to 1966. He has written hundreds of articles and is the author of a book called "Cowboy in the Classroom."

## Don Evans — Acceptance Speech

Greetings to the current FHS faculty and students... to my friends in attendance tonight... to my fellow inductees... and especially to Mary Jane Masters and Edith McGlaughlin. Don't let the name "David Evans" confuse you. At FHS I was known by my middle name, Donald (or even "Donnie" by my family and many of my peers).

I am terribly sorry that I cannot be here in person... the demands of my work are particularly intense at this time of year.

We underestimate the degree to which one's high school education and experience influences the future path of its students. I, for one, attribute much of my career success to the profound influence of several teachers, including Mary Jane and Edith, who were outstandingly competent teachers.

Because of my bent towards math and science, however, I was influenced most decisively by Louise Lyons, who personally challenged me to do well in Mathematics, a subject which led to college degrees in science and in engineering. This path eventually provided me with the opportunity to participate in the exciting world of space exploration, exploring the planets in our solar system as well as our home planet, earth.

I fondly remember these teachers, and many others too numerous to name, as well as all 55 of my classmates. These individuals also provided me with the opportunity to develop my leadership skills through Student government service, skills which have served me well throughout my career.

I will always have a soft spot in my heart for my original "home-town," Farmington. Both my Father and Mother are buried here, and my brother, Carroll, and Sister, Lenora Brothers, are still Farmington residents. They are both here tonight, and I appreciate their support.

I wish to sincerely thank the faculty and administration of the High School for instituting this recognition program, and want especially to thank the Hall of Fame committee for your kind consideration. I am aware of the achievements of my co-inductees, and I am honored to be a member of this outstanding group.

Again, my apologies for not being here to personally deliver this message.

Thank you and Good Evening.

Don Evans
