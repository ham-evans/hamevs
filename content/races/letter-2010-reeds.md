December 13, 2010

Hi Dick and Thelma,

First of all don't faint! I am actually writing you a note this Christmas. I received your letter today and decided that I would immediately respond since I am very good at procrastinating. (I practice it.)

I will give you a short synopsis of what we have been up to recently. I was duly impressed with your description of Daniel and Melinda and their various activities. We don't have anything like that and our family but I will have a chance to brag a bit later. And yes I do remember all of your offspring, but of course much has changed since I last saw you guys.

Our oldest son, Tim, lives in South Pasadena in a beautiful old 1920s home. He and Karen have three children. The two oldest, Kate and Hamilton, are enrolled at Flintridge prep, a private school which is located very close to our former home in La Canada. They are both good students and both run cross-country and track much like their dad did. Their youngest son, Sebastian, goes to a local middle school and is a sports-nut. We do get over to South Pasadena occasionally, although not much recently, to visit them. Tim is an attorney at a down town law firm, and Karen teaches part time at local schools.

Our other son, Bruce, is the VP of product development for a Internet startup firm that has been generously funded by one of the investment Angels. Bruce and Anita have three sons and a daughter. Just like Shauna, Anita homeschooled all four of these until they were old enough for middle school. And they all became proficient on the piano. They all attended a Christian school located near their home in Moscow, Idaho. All of these kids are very bright; the two younger boys maintaining a perfect 4.0 average so far. The oldest, Tyler, graduated from high school in 2009 at the top of his class and did extremely well on the SATs. (This is the bragging part.) He managed a perfect score on the mathematics portion at a very high score on the balance of the SATs. I thought I was pretty good at math but I could never have accomplished what he did. As a result of this, along with his various activities and skills at writing he was offered a full-ride scholarship in the honor college at Alabama University, and had other offers as well. He also won a cash stipend from an Alabama benefactor for students in the engineering field. In other words he is getting paid to go to college.

Our daughter, Marily, lives in a condominium about 10 min. away from us. As you may recall she is the one that was born with spinal Bifida, which affected her lower legs and thus ability to walk normally. She is now in a wheelchair and thus requires a bit of assistance from us at least currently. She was able to drive her car previously but now is learning to use hand controls. She does enjoy her little bulldog, Gus. (We do too.) Nancy and I are the support system for her.

Last year I resigned from active duty, if you will, with the AARP Tax Aide program. I am still on a subcommittee, but this does not require much time. And I gave up doing taxes for people, although I didn't do a lot of that in the past. Hence I spend time doing the usual keep-up-the house stuff, messing around with my computer, which I thoroughly enjoy, and tinkering and piddling as Dick would say.

Lastly, I would like to complement David on what is an amazing performance in the triathlon. Please keep me posted on how he does in China. However, I don't understand how he could be over 50 years old. I don't even want to think about how old you and I are getting. I certainly don't feel like I'm approaching 80, and I guess that's what really counts anyway.

Thanks for the nice letter and do stay in touch. Enjoy the Christmas season and next year as well!
