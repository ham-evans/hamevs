*Use Their Imagination!*

The secret to bedtime storytelling is to tailor the story to the age and understanding of the child, which many of you probably already do. This guide is intended to give one some hints based around a few basic stories which I have found have worked well with my children and my grandchildren. They begged to be told a "Pupsie" story most every night.

Each story should be told in such a manner that the child's mind can easily create their own image of what is happening. This approach differs considerably from standard illustrated children's story books which provide their own set story and illustrations for the child to absorb, leaving less for the imagination. Later we will discuss how to adapt basic stories to each child since their knowledge may be limited with very young children.

## A Basic Story Line

Let's start with one of the basic stories; a story about Pupsie the dog, which never comes out exactly the same each time, since I would modify it based on the age of the children and also change the storyline so that it would not be so scary for the little ones. My children would often beg me to tell them a Pupsie story as they were tucked into bed. After you have read through the short story we will do a quick analysis and I will make some comments that may be helpful as you construct your own stories.

## Pupsie the Dog

> *Pupsie was a little puppy dog that lived in a small doghouse with his two brothers and two sisters. His daddy and his mommy had both gone out to play in the big yard. Mommy told him that all the puppies needed to stay in their small yard and not go out because there may be bad things out there that might hurt them. Well, there was a fence around the small yard meant to keep in the dogs anyway.*
>
> *One day Pupsie was feeling really good and had heard from some of the birds that there were fun things to do outside of the small yard. So he told his brothers and sisters that he thought he just might try to get through the fence and go out there to explore. His brothers and sisters were quite concerned and said to him "Pupsie, don't do that. Mommy said we were to stay in the small yard!"*
>
> *But Pupsie was a little naughty. He decided he really wanted to go exploring and said he would try to sneak out and do that while mommy dog was gone. He found a place under the fence, dug down a little bit, and squirmed under until he was on the outside of the small yard. He felt pretty good about this. There was a big world out there with all kinds of things to sniff at and chase after.*
>
> *He could see a nice flat green meadow not too far away so he trotted over to it as fast as he could. He could see the bees buzzing around and the butterflies flitting around, and could hear the birds cheeping. But then he noticed some tall trees on the other side of the meadow that looked like an interesting place to go. He started walking toward the trees. But about that time a friendly owl flew down and said to him "Don't go into the forest. A big bear lives there!"*
>
> *Well, Pupsie did not really know what a big bear was and anyway he was too excited to be outside in the big world so he just said "I'll be really careful and besides I really want to explore out here — it looks like fun!" So he trotted off toward the trees.*
>
> *When he first entered the tall trees it looked like it would be really fun, but as he went further into it it got kind of dark because the sun couldn't get through the trees very well. He slowed down to a slow walk and kept going. The owl flew down again and said be very careful now since it's getting kind of dark in here.*
>
> *All the sudden behind a large tree there was a big black bear — he was up on his hind feet so he could see well. Well, Pupsie was concerned about this since the bear was such a big tall animal but he didn't know what to do. The bear moved closer and closer but finally he stopped and said to Pupsie "Don't be afraid, I'm a very friendly bear." Pupsie looked up at the bear and said "Well, I'm a very friendly puppy." The bear went on to say "However, you do need to be careful since not all bears are friendly like me. I think you should go home to your mommy and daddy and stay out of this forest with tall trees."*
>
> *Now Pupsie remembered what the owl said also, so he put his little tail between his legs and scampered off out of the forest back to the fence, under the fence and back with his brothers and sisters. As soon as he saw them he said "WHEW!" He was so glad to be back with his family, and they lived happily ever after.*
