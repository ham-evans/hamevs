This short story happened as best I can recollect during 1944, which was certainly during an active time during World War II.

At the time I was seriously interested in military aviation and eagerly followed the news stories of the war and the brief excerpts shown in our local theater. As an example I had read and reread the story "30 seconds over Tokyo" many times. We lived in Farmington, New Mexico, a small town in northwest New Mexico with a population of approximately 3000 people. We had a small airport at the top of the mesa near the town but the only aircraft that we would see coming and going were small light planes used for either recreation or business purposes. This airport had a single East West runway of about 4000 feet. We were at elevation of approximately 5200 feet, so a longer runway was needed for many larger aircraft. I believe the runway was paved but I'm unsure of that.

One summer day a military P-38 started circling the town at a very low altitude. We all ran out of the house to see what was going on. I had instantly recognized the twin tails of the P-38 and of course the noise from the twin engines was something to behold for us. Much to our surprise we could see that this aircraft had entered a pattern and was landing in our small airport on the mesa. This was exciting!

My friends and I caught a ride with one of the parents to go up and see this magnificent bird and the Air Force pilot. Well, by the time we got there the local police had set up a perimeter around the aircraft to protect it, however we did get a good look at it and we got to see the pilot as well. I had never seen a P-38 except in newsreels and that kind of thing. It turned out that this pilot became disoriented as we say (a nice way of saying lost), and he was running low on fuel.

We found out that a fuel truck was being dispatched from Albuquerque so he could be refueled and could fly back out. We arranged to be notified when the fuel truck arrived so we could come up and watch the takeoff. The following day this happened. We stood in awe as the pilot started up the engines, taxied out and took off. For us this was an ultimate excitement for at least a month! He made one turn as he climbed out and flew back over the city. This was not a low pass. He was either showing off a bit or he was just trying to get a heading to Albuquerque. We talked about this for weeks after that.

<p style="margin-bottom:0.25rem"><em>David Donald Evans</em></p>
<p style="margin-top:0"><em>August 23, 2011</em></p>
