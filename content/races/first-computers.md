## The Atari 800 — November 1982

Purchased November 5, 1982 from Olympic Sales Company, Inc., 1756 E. Colorado Blvd., Pasadena, CA 91101.

- Atari 800 — $420
- Disk Drive — $75 (with cass?)
- Disks — $29
- **Total: $1,216.36**

*Atari 800 Purchase with 48K of Memory!*

The Atari 800 Home Computer worked with any television set or video monitor. It was powerful and expandable — you could add accessories to build the computer system you needed. It came with the Atari 810 Disk Drive for fast storage and the Atari 410 Program Recorder for cassette tape storage.

Grandave also picked up peripherals from Microbits Peripheral Products (MPP) of Albany, Oregon:

- **MPP-1000C Modem** — $149.95. Auto answer/auto dial, direct connect to phone line, free 1 hour subscription to "THE SOURCE," free CompuServe DemoPak, connects to joystick port. Supported XMODEM protocol, ASCII/ATASCII translation, multiple buffers, off-line editing.

- **MPP-1150 Printer Interface** — $99.95. Replaced the Atari 850 Interface Module, compatible with Epson, NEC, Prowriter printers.

- **MicroFiler** — $49.95. "A truly practical filing program! Makes your family Atari a tool, not a toy!" Used for Christmas card lists, recipes, expense accounts, collections. "After your kids have destroyed the universe, you can find Aunt Martha's recipe for German Chocolate Cake, or balance your check book... in seconds!"

The Atari 400/800 BASIC Reference Manual was the programming guide — Grandave enjoyed exploring and programming on the computer, as mentioned in his 1983 Christmas letter.

## The 486 PC — March 1994

Handwritten note on the brochure: *"My First Real Computer — March, 1994"*

Purchased from New Technologies, 461 North Lake Avenue, Pasadena, CA 91101.

**NT 486 WinSeries — "Windows Computing at its best!"**

Specs:
- Processor: 486DX33/DX2-50/DX2-66 MHz with integrated Math-Co
- Memory: 4 MB (exp. to 64MB)
- Cache: 128KB (exp. to 512KB)
- Hard Drive: 340MB Fast IDE
- Diskette Drive: 3.5" 1.44 MB
- Slots: 6 full size including 3 VLB and 3 ISA
- Storage Bays: 4 available — two 5.25", two 3.5"
- VLB video adapters: CL5428 with 1 MB DRAM (exp. to 2 MB)
- Ports: 2 Serial, 1 Parallel, 1 Game port
- Input devices: 3-button mouse, 101 enhanced keyboard
- System/Power: Mini Tower case with 220 Watt power supply
- Display: 14" SVGA Color Monitor
- Operating System: MicroSoft DOS 6.2 and MicroSoft Windows 3.1

**Invoice — Sold to David Evans, 417 Woodfield, La Canada, CA 91011**

This is for a 486 Computer with Disk Drive, Mouse, and 4MB of Memory.

**Total was $1,677 in March, 1994!**

Items on the invoice:
- 486 PC with 72-pin 4MB Memory Module — $1,350
- 1.2MB Floppy Drive — $50
- Simms — $64 (?)
- Ergonomic Mouse V. 3.0 OEM — $35
