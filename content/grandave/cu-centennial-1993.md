### CU Engineering Centennial, 1893–1993 — 100 Years of Excellence

You are cordially invited to the opening event of the CU College of Engineering and Applied Science's Centennial Celebration, a commemoration of 100 years of excellence.

Please join us for the College's Centennial Symposium on September 30 and October 1, 1993.

**Thursday, September 30**

1:00 to 5:00 p.m. — Departmental and general seminars. The common theme will be current issues in engineering and engineering education. Engineering Center (Rooms for the seminars will be posted.)

**Friday, October 1**

9:30 a.m. to Noon — "The Future of Engineering," a plenary talk by Norman R. Augustine, Chairman and CEO, Martin Marietta Corporation. Panel will include MIT Professor Sheila E. Widnall, nominated by the President to be the next Secretary of the Air Force. Macky Auditorium.

12:30 to 2:00 p.m. — Picnic lunch and entertainment. Folsom Park and Gemmill Engineering Library patio (east of the Engineering Center)

2:00 to 4:00 p.m. — College and departmental tours

### Speaker Biographies

**Prof. K. D. Wood** — First Chair of Aerospace Engineering Sciences, served from 1947 to 1956, and again from 1957–1962.

**Dr. Robert Wood** (B.S., AERO, CU-1949) — Responsible for advanced development on the space station, and continues to work on "after the space station, what next?" for McDonnell Douglas Aerospace. Was Chief Space Research Engineer at Douglas Aircraft Co. in 1961, and instrumental in developing quality standards for R&D. Also was Director of R&D for all of the missiles and space programs at McDonnell Douglas in Huntington Beach, from 1977–84.

**David D. Evans** (B.S., AERO, CU-1958) — Deputy Assistant Laboratory Director of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory's Flight Projects. Involved in several ongoing JPL projects: Voyager, Magellan, Galileo, Ulysses, Mars Observer, Topex/Poseidon (all in operations phase); and Cassini and MESUR (under development).

**Vance Brand** (B.S., AERO, CU-1960) — ASTRONAUT. Graduated with a Business degree in 1953 from CU, returning to earn a B.S. in Aero in 1960. In July of 1975, he was involved in the Apollo-Soyuz mission. Twice flew on Space Shuttle Columbia (Nov. 1982 and Dec. 1990), and also was an astronaut aboard the Space Shuttle CHALLENGER in February 1984.

**James Voss** (M.S., AERO CU-1974) — ASTRONAUT. An Aerospace Engineering Sciences graduate, Voss has flown on Space Shuttles ATLANTIS (11/91) and COLUMBIA (10/92).

**Colonel R. H. Worrell, III** (M.S., AERO, CU-1983) — Director, National Test Bed/National Test Facility, Air Force Space Command. Vietnam experience in 1969 as a forward air controller with the 21st Tactical Air Support Squadron; flew 368 missions. From 1974–1981, Aircraft Commander or instruction pilot and flight examiner at Cannon Air Force Base, New Mexico. Received the Air Force Daedalian Foundation Fellowship in 1979 and attended CU, earning his master's in 1983. Also served at Peterson Air Force Base, Colorado, as Chief, Consolidated Space Support Division and Chief, Training Development Division. Col. Worrell has more than 3,500 flying hours.

**Future of Engineering**
- Hard to imagine technical tools that will be available; readiness/judgement will be even more important
- Challenge will not be as much on technical, but instead on blending technical progress with social needs (much more interaction than we see now)

  - Recycle ease & utility
  - Environmental impact — initial point

### Presentation Slides — "Dedicated Behavior"

**Slide 1:**
- The willingness on the part of the individual to do whatever it takes to advance the interests of the wider organization regardless of the internal organizational boundaries.

**Slide 2:**
- Awareness that the real leverage of organization comes from the totality of effort.
- Willingness to guide one's daily efforts in a way that reinforces the aims of the whole organization.
