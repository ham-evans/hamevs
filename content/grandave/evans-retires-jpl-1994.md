*La Cañada Valley Sun, June 1994 — by Shirley DeGrey*

![Evans receives commemorative titanium plaque from JPL Chief Engineer John Casani](/races/evans-retires-jpl-1994.jpg)

<figcaption>RETIRES — La Cañadan David D. Evans, acting assistant Propulsion Laboratory director for flight projects, receives a commemorative titanium plaque from the jet lab's Chief Engineer John Casani, showing spacecraft and projects Evans worked on during his 36-year JPL career.</figcaption>

CalTech Jet Propulsion Laboratory colleagues, friends and family gathered at Von Karman Auditorium recently for an afternoon reception marking the retirement of David D. Evans, acting assistant laboratory director for JPL flight projects, after a distinguished career which began in 1958 when he joined JPL as a research engineer.

Chief Engineer John Casani was master of ceremonies, introducing a number of associates, past and present, who shared anecdotes and recollections of Evans' 36-year career.

Kirk Dawson, associate director, presented the Jet Propulsion Laboratory retirement plaque.

Casani presented a large framed titanium plaque with an engraved map showing the various spacecraft and projects worked on by Evans.

Other presentations included scrapbooks, photographs, plaques and gifts.

Special guests at the event included Evans' wife, Nancy, a former JPL missile project mathematician, whom he met shortly after he started to work there. They married and moved to the La Cañada Flintridge in 1961, raising two sons and a daughter. Son Tim and Karen Evans of Phoenix were not present, but son Bruce and Anita Evans and their young children Tyler, two, and Kenzie, nine months and daughter Marily Evans attended.

In his responding remarks, Dave Evans said that "One of the best things about working at JPL has been that I've lived five minutes away from my job." He will be on call, doing occasional consulting at the lab.

A native of Farmington, N.M., Evans earned a bachelor of science degree in chemistry in 1953 from the University of New Mexico, and a second B.S., degree in aeronautical engineering in 1958 from the University of Colorado. From 1953–1956 he was a jet fighter pilot in the U.S. Air Force.

During his career at JPL, Evans worked in such divisions as Combustion Devices Group, Liquid Propulsion Section, and Control Energy Conversion Division. He managed the Pathfinder/Space Relay Experiment Projects, JPL's Defense Program, and Mars Observer program.
