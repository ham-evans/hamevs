## Transcription

D. Don Evans
417 Woodfield Rd.
La Canada, Calif. 91011

*For All Who Missed It
And To Up-date All Who Experienced It*

The Farmington High School graduating class of 1949 celebrated their first ever reunion on June 22 and 23, 1984. Our 35th anniversary of exiting the halls of learning at FHS.

Why were you not there? Different reasons for different persons, but we will give you another chance in 1989. It's just five years until the 40th year out — WOW!

The attached list will give you the addresses that we have on file and data from letters and from gossip (which may or may not be correct, please excuse if not and fill us in) — if any of you happen to move in the next 4 years or so, please let Don Conley or someone here in Farmington know as plans are already in the works for the next reunion.

The reunion got started with a bang at a get-together at Raymond and Patsy Kysar's house. Those attending having had a head start on recognizing the others the next day at the picnic. A family picnic was held at the park with lots of good visiting and comparing notes for the past 35 years. Pictures were taken and for those of you who missed out, we'll be glad to show you ours at the next reunion. The last party of the weekend was the dinner-dance at the Country Club on Saturday night. The food was superb, the music nostalgic, and the program helped us all get caught up on the news. A good time was had by all!

For those of you unaware of it, Classmate Gussie Whitener Saleh died on the day of our reunion, June 23, 1984, from a brain tumor. She had very much wanted to be at the reunion and had written to Don Conley about it — a copy of her letter is enclosed. This brings to four the total of classmates no longer with us. In addition to Gussie, there are Nelda Stacey, Preston Townsend and Natalie Irvin who have passed away.

A special thanks should go to those in Farmington who worked so long and hard to put this together — especially Don Conley.

Keep in touch! And we'll see you in five years!

## Letter from Gussie Whitener Saleh

*June 19, 1984*

Dear Donald,

I'm sorry to miss the re-union and after this sad profile on a substitute letter. First let me say the spirit was willing but the body really failed. I think one of the best touches and kindest either of you could possibly have provided. Of course they sent right because it would have been too easy not to, but I still respect the initiative which they produced for me, from coming. I sure you guys have worked hard on the re-union and I sincerely hope it's a huge success. Being able to say I was a 1949 member of Farmington and in the ranks of the Navy Base October 1950, an escort along with Lt. Commander outside the ranks of the Navy Base October 1950, an escort along with us.

From high school I went to San Francisco to attend beauty college for nine months and worked there for about two years. I married in 1952 and moved to Farmington. It serves my purpose best that we lived in eight different California locales in 1956 and in one year, we in San Diego, I just finished right at the six year point. After 14 months in Las Vegas during the Contonment and Viet Nam, we then moved to a house which I have been in since. In 1982 while we were on a shore duty status, we got transferred to a three year tour, two of them in the beautiful state of Hawaii and it's related possession war in order. We spent several California years alone and I have an RV of license and am looking to sell it. Unfortunately, while I was able to make Hong Kong (Formosa-Taipei City), Taiwan, Philippines, Japan, and so on, my happiest experience was in the United Kingdom in 1981, where our home base as Air Force base, about thirty five miles out of San Francisco. We spent three years there and thoroughly enjoyed the slower pace and the opportunity for sightseeing again. Then came the last orders of the English — their retirement orders, and now we accept it, it was the greatest gift the Navy could have given us. Retirement allowed and required us to go to England, Germany, France, Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Spain, Scotland, and I think I was even invited to visit the Queen, but I guess I wasn't royal enough for that, and they are probably still wondering if I did it. Actually, they just didn't want to invite my husband since his temperament and our lifestyle was clearly not their cup of English tea!

After 4 years we settled in San Diego in 1982 and worked as a computer serving six pages of service. We retired as a Commander having seen half the world. He got a job with Rohr almost right away after retirement and moved to San Antonio, TX in 1979. It was one of the wisest decisions we could possibly have made in our life. We became involved in the oil industry, getting some fine people as friends, and joined the San Antonio Historical Society, getting involved in restoration, auction, Fiesta Porvenir tours and concerts and San Antonio culture. In 1982 I was severely hurt in an auto accident. I was in a bad physical condition for about three years but have recovered considerably. I'm still in some pain daily, and still have some back problems, but I function as normally as possible. My husband and I have tried to keep an active social life. In my case, at least, the social activities kept me from going stale. Unfortunately, all has not been peaches and cream since the accident. As we know, the oil economy has been in a serious decline for about a year. We still have our residence, but like many others in our area, we have had to make some real adjustments to our lifestyle. I really hope it all works out. I'm not complaining and I'm certainly not discouraged, my spirits are generally very good and I'm very grateful that I am alive.

I have two grandchildren, a girl of two years and a boy just three months old. My daughter is 28 and I've yet to see him, but that is his loss. He is to be stationed later this summer, the lifestyle was divided until August 1980 when I was diagnosed as having a brain tumor. I was operated on at the neurological surgery center at Wilford Hall at Lackland Air Force Base. Two additional operations have been performed to correct the tumor's seizures that resulted. At the present time we have managed to stabilize the seizures with high doses of Dilantin and Phenobarbital. I think it is remarkable that I have gotten to their dosage. My best days are six a time, enjoying it fully, and thankful for the rest. And listen, I'd give a lot to make the re-union because it was one of my goals, and I am very proud. Please send me whatever photographs you get copies of it. It's good to know that there are some very good old regards and of course we need to do that sometime. Please drop by, I'd love to see you and please make sure that you or someone send a lot of them with everyone at my Farmington P.O. number (1112 Post Office). It will be a wonderful time. May happiness be your effort, Please make believe they me a line at the end.

Gussie
